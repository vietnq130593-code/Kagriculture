Harvested items are added to the inventory. </li>\n<li>FERTILIZE — Fertilize a plant to increase its potential yield (see harvest yields below).  <ul>\n<li>Doubles the per-day yield bonus for the next 3 days. In the case of animals they escape (unrecoverable).</p>\n<p>A new seed starts with <code>consecutive_unwatered = 1</code> — the planting day itself counts as the first missed day. The base yield is 1 per scheduled production. If the plant is fertilized AND watered that day, yield is doubled to 2.  </li>\n<li>Once a plant has hit its maximum lifespan, the total yield available on the plant will reduce by 1 every other turn until it hits 0, at which point the plant becomes a weed.<ul>\n<li><strong>One-time crops</strong> reach max lifespan one day after <code>max_yield_day</code>.</li>\n<li><strong>Ongoing crops</strong> start decay one day after their cumulative production count reaches <code>max_yield</code> (i.e. they've fired enough scheduled productions to hit the cap, regardless of whether the produce has been harvested).</li></ul></li>\n</ul>\n<h2>Map Features</h2>\n<p>Each player has their own farm with a set number of squares. At first, your farm covers one quadrant (25% of the squares). For an increasingly large fee, you can buy the neighboring quadrants and eventually cover 100% of the squares.  </li>\n<li>Each plant or animal occupies one square on the farm.  </li>\n<li>Players can allocate these squares however they choose between crops and livestock. There are no specific limits per type.  </li>\n<li>Weeds have a chance of spawning on any empty cells on the farm, and must be cleared before the land can be used for other purposes.  </li>\n<li>Squares on the farm can be either a plant, a coop/pasture, a weed, or empty.</li>\n</ul>\n<h3>Shed (Inventory)</h3>\n<ul>\n<li>Functions as an inventory for items that are harvested but not yet sold, or for seeds that have not yet been planted  </li>\n<li>Farmer and hired farm hands will spawn at the shed at the start of each day  </li>\n<li>Farmer and hired farm hands drop their inventory at the end of the day in the shed (if there is room)  </li>\n<li>Limited to 100 items, excluding seeds. Once the shed is full, any further items added (via <code>PLACE</code> mid-day or end-of-day inventory drop) are discarded — there is no overflow holding area, so stockpiling on farmer/hand inventories does not bypass the cap.</li>\n</ul>\n<p>The shed sits at the center of the board and is not a tile — it never appears in the <code>tiles</code> array, whose only values are <code>None</code>, <code>\"LOCKED\"</code>, and structure dicts. \"Orthogonally adjacent to the shed\" means standing on one of the four center tiles, <code>(half-1, half-1)</code>, <code>(half, half-1)</code>, <code>(half-1, half)</code>, <code>(half, half)</code> for <code>half = boardSize // 2</code>. At the default <code>boardSize = 10</code> those are <code>(4,4)</code>, <code>(5,4)</code>, <code>(4,5)</code>, and <code>(5,5)</code>, one in each quadrant. Since only NW starts unlocked, three of those four tiles begin locked; the shed is reachable from all of them regardless, because the shed itself is never locked.</p>\n<h3>Farmer/Farm Hand</h3>\n<h4>Hiring</h4>\n<ul>\n<li>Hiring is a market order (<code>HIRE</code>). It costs more every time you want to hire an additional hand each day. At the end of the day all, hands drop inventory at the farm and disappear (need to be re-hired each day)  </li>\n<li>Cost is <code>farmHandCostMult * fib(n)</code> where <code>n</code> is the number of hires already made today (fib starts 1, 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13, …).  <ul>\n<li>With the default <code>farmHandCostMult = 1</code>: 1, 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13, 21, etc… (resets at the start of each day)  </li></ul></li>\n<li>A hired hand appears orthogonally adjacent to the shed in a free space following NWSE. If there are not open spaces, it looks for the one with the least occupants, breaking ties by NWSE preference</li>\n<li>Spawn placement ignores whether the tile is locked. Since the main farmer starts on <code>(4,4)</code>, the least-occupied rule sends the first hire of each day to <code>(5,4)</code>, which is locked until the NE quadrant is bought. Locked tiles are passable, so a hand spawned on one can move back to unlocked land.</li>\n</ul>\n<h4>Inventory</h4>\n<ul>\n<li>When harvesting or picking items up, they are added to inventory.  </li>\n<li>Can drop items in the shed  </li>\n<li>At the end of the day, all items in all inventory will be added to shed inventory (if there is room). Anything that doesn't fit is discarded — overflow is lost.</li>\n</ul>\n<h3>Town Buildings</h3>\n<p>As the season progresses, new shops unlock at regular intervals (every <code>townShopUnlockInterval</code> days, default 3). Each unlock is drawn uniformly at random <strong>with replacement</strong> from the full shop table, so the same shop can unlock more than once — a season might end up with three bakeries and no yarn store. Once unlocked, a shop stays active for the rest of the game, and unlocking stops after 8 total instances. Total demand grows monotonically as more shops unlock.</p>\n<p>Each unlocked shop <em>instance</em> consumes one of every product it demands every <code>townShopSellInterval</code> turns (default 4). So with the default interval, a shop demanding wheat removes 6 wheat from the market per day, and two copies of that shop remove 12. Single-product shops consume 2x.</p>\n<p>In addition, the town center consumes one of every product (excluding fertilizer) every <code>townCenterSellInterval</code> turns (default 24, i.e. once per day). This rate is flat for the whole season — it does not ramp.</p>\n<table>\n<thead>\n<tr>\n<th>Shop Type</th>\n<th>Increases Demand For</th>\n</tr>\n</thead>\n<tbody>\n<tr>\n<td>Bakery</td>\n<td>eggs, wheat</td>\n</tr>\n<tr>\n<td>Pizza Shop</td>\n<td>milk, tomatoes, wheat</td>\n</tr>\n<tr>\n<td>Brunch Spot</td>\n<td>eggs, wheat, strawberries</td>\n</tr>\n<tr>\n<td>Yarn Store</td>\n<td>wool (2x)</td>\n</tr>\n<tr>\n<td>Ice Cream Shop</td>\n<td>strawberries, milk, wheat</td>\n</tr>\n<tr>\n<td>Pet Cafe</td>\n<td>carrots (2x)</td>\n</tr>\n<tr>\n<td>Smoothie Shop</td>\n<td>strawberries, milk</td>\n</tr>\n<tr>\n<td>Farmers Market</td>\n<td>wheat, carrots, tomatoes, strawberries</td>\n</tr>\n</tbody>\n</table>\n<h2>Market Mechanics</h2>\n<p>The market has an unlimited supply of seeds and animals at fixed prices. Sell prices, however, move dynamically per resource and persist across days.</p>\n<p>Every product (and fertilizer) starts the game with a market inventory of <code>I0 = 10,000</code> units, far above any single game's realistic production volume so that inventory is essentially guaranteed to stay positive. The sell price for a product is <code>base</code> at <code>I0</code>, rises as inventory falls (players buying or town consumption draining supply), and falls as inventory grows (players selling).</p>\n<h3>Selling inventory to the market</h3>\n<p>Players can queue any number of sell or buy orders (for any quantity) in the market action list. Orders are processed concurrently across players, one unit at a time. For example, when both players issue <code>SELL CARROT 10</code> first, we take the current carrot price, give both players that price for their first carrot, then add 2 carrots to the market (1 from each player) — which may shift the price — and repeat until both orders complete.</p>\n<p>If the sell price has been driven down to <code>$1</code> (the price floor), the unit is still purchased but is <em>not</em> added to market inventory, so the floor remains responsive to subsequent buys.</p>\n<h3>Buying inventory from the market</h3>\n<p>Only <code>WHEAT</code> and <code>FERTILIZER</code> can be bought from the market via <code>BUY_PRODUCT</code> (other products are sold at the market but not bought back). Selling is unrestricted: every product, including fertilizer collected from animals, can be sold via <code>SELL</code>. Two things drain market inventory: town buildings (town center and shops, which consume products for free) and player <code>BUY_PRODUCT</code> orders. Buy orders follow the same one-unit-at-a-time concurrent procedure as sell orders. If a player runs out of money mid-order, the order is stopped.</p>\n<p>The buy price is quoted at the post-buy inventory and the sell price is quoted at the pre-sell inventory, so an immediate buy followed by a sell of the same item against an otherwise-unchanged market nets exactly zero.</p>\n<h3>The Price Function</h3>\n<p>For each resource the curve is defined by a base price, an anchor throughput <code>T</code>, and an independent <strong>shape function</strong> + <strong>target move</strong> for each side of the equilibrium:</p>\n<pre class=\"uc-code-block\"><code id=\"code-block-1788806196557-0\" data-processing=\"yes\" data-highlighted=\"yes\" class=\"hljs language-dns\">price<span class=\"hljs-punctuation\">(</span>inv<span class=\"hljs-punctuation\">)</span> = base + sign · amp · f<span class=\"hljs-punctuation\">(</span>|inv − I0|<span class=\"hljs-punctuation\">)</span>\n  sign = +<span class=\"hljs-number\">1</span>  if inv &lt; Below <code>T</code> it is linear in <code>u</code>; above <code>T</code> the quadratic term takes over and the price climbs steeply. Since <code>f(T) = 1</code> by construction, <code>target</code> keeps its usual meaning.</p>\n<p><code>T</code> is the production capacity of a single 5×5 field over a 24-day window at optimal watering with no fertilizer (animal totals are pre-discounted by 30% to account for wheat-feed overhead, and allow one day to build the coop or pasture). The 24-day window is a calibration horizon, not the 30-day season length. It is shorter on purpose: the opening days are setup-heavy and yield little.</p>\n<p><code>target</code> says \"moving <code>T</code> units past <code>I0</code> shifts the price by <code>target × base</code>.\" Picking different <code>f</code> and <code>target</code> on each side lets resources with similar production profiles play very differently strategically — wheat panics on scarcity but absorbs gluts; melon barely reacts to scarcity but crashes hard on overproduction; wool mirrors melon at a smaller scale. Premium resources (base &gt; $100: strawberry, melon, milk, wool) use <code>above_target &gt; 1</code>, so even modest gluts drive them straight to the $1 floor — bundling and timing sales matters more for these than for staples.</p>\n<p>Carrot, tomato and egg use <code>hinge</code> on the scarcity side, so their prices stay near base under ordinary demand and rise sharply once demand runs past <code>T</code>. The town shops that consume them are listed in <code>unlocked_shops</code>.</p>\n<ul>\n<li><strong>Carrot</strong> — <code>hinge</code> at <code>below_target</code> 1.00.
